# SOC 2 and the Trust Services Criteria

## 1. Quick concept

SOC 2 is an examination and reporting service for service organizations. An independent CPA examines controls relevant to selected Trust Services Criteria (TSC). The criteria cover Security (often called the Common Criteria) and, where relevant to the engagement, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, and Privacy. Select scope and criteria based on the service, customer needs, and risks.

Official starting point: [AICPA SOC resources](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/topic/audit-assurance/audit-and-assurance-greater-than-soc-2) and [Trust Services Criteria with revised points of focus](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/download/2017-trust-services-criteria-with-revised-points-of-focus-2022).

## 2. Type 1 vs. Type 2

- **Type 1:** reports on the description of the system and suitability of control design as of a specified date.
- **Type 2:** also evaluates operating effectiveness over a defined period.

A SOC 2 report is not an ISO-style certification. Review the system description, scope, opinion, report period, exceptions, complementary user entity controls, subservice organization treatment, and any carve-outs or qualifications.

## 3. Enterprise workflow

1. Define the service, system boundary, customer commitments, and relevant TSC.
2. Map criteria to control activities and assign control owners.
3. Design controls, define evidence, and operate them consistently before the examination period.
4. Perform readiness testing and resolve gaps.
5. Work with the CPA firm on scope, period, sampling, request lists, and interviews.
6. Provide traceable evidence and respond to follow-up questions.
7. Review report exceptions and management responses accurately.
8. Maintain controls and track bridge-period changes or customer-specific questions.

## 4. Example control and evidence

**Assertion:** production changes are authorized, tested, reviewed, and tracked.

**Evidence:** change procedure; a complete population of releases/changes from the system of record; auditor-selected tickets; peer-review approvals; test results; deployment logs; emergency-change rationale and post-review.

A polished ticket does not demonstrate that all in-scope production changes followed the process. The CPA determines appropriate procedures and sampling.

## 5. Day-to-day work

Maintain control descriptions and owners; track evidence deadlines; validate source exports; coordinate walkthroughs; manage exceptions and remediation; review customer questionnaires against the actual report; and share confidential reports under approved terms.

## 6. Common pitfalls

- Saying “SOC 2 certified.” More accurate wording is that an organization has a SOC 2 report for a defined scope and period.
- Treating Type 1 as proof of year-round operating effectiveness.
- Ignoring complementary user entity controls the customer must implement.
- Ignoring report scope, period, exceptions, or subservice providers.
- Sharing a report outside contractual/confidentiality conditions.

## 7. Interview cheat sheet

**Q: A customer sends a vendor SOC 2 report. What do you review?** Confirm report type, service/system, period, CPA opinion, exceptions, user-entity controls, subservice treatment and scope, criteria selected, and remediation evidence. Assess whether the report covers the service you use; do not treat it as universal approval.

**Q: Why does a Type 2 report cover a period?** Operating effectiveness is evaluated over a defined period using the examination's procedures and samples. Period, scope, exceptions, and opinion shape the assurance provided.
