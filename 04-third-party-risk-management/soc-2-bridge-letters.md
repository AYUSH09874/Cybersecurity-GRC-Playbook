# SOC 2 Bridge Letters: What They Do and Do Not Prove

## 1. Quick concept

A SOC 2 bridge letter is commonly a management-provided communication about the period after a SOC 2 report ends and before a later report is available. It may describe management's awareness of material changes or control matters in that gap. Its format and assurance level vary; it is **not a substitute for an independent SOC 2 examination** and is not a new auditor's opinion.

Review the actual letter, report, contract, and customer/auditor requirements. Not every organization is required to accept a bridge letter.

## 2. Review checklist

- Is the underlying report authentic and relevant to the service being purchased?
- What is the report end date, and what gap does the letter cover?
- Who signed it and in what management capacity?
- What does it actually assert, and what limitations are stated?
- Does it address incidents, control changes, acquisitions, regions, or subservice organizations?
- Is a newer report, interim testing, or alternative assurance available?
- Is the gap material given the vendor's criticality, data, access, and obligations?
- Who accepted any residual risk, and until when?

## 3. Example workflow

A critical SaaS vendor's SOC 2 Type 2 report ended six months ago. TPRM checks that the report covers the purchased service, reviews exceptions and user-entity controls, obtains the bridge letter, asks about incidents/material changes, and checks contract commitments. Security and Legal assess significance; an authorized business risk owner decides whether to renew, approve conditionally, or request more assurance. GRC records the basis and next follow-up date.

## 4. Pitfalls

- Treating a bridge letter as independent auditor testing of the gap period.
- Ignoring exceptions in the underlying report.
- Accepting a generic letter with no dates, scope, or accountable signatory.
- Failing to track when the next report is expected.

## 5. Interview question

**Q: Can you say a vendor remained compliant throughout the gap based on the bridge letter?** Not from the letter alone. State what management represents, review the underlying report and exceptions, assess changes and incidents, confirm service/period, request more evidence if warranted, and document the residual-risk decision. Do not describe a management letter as an independent examination.
