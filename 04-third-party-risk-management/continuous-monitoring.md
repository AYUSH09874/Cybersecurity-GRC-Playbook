# Continuous Third-Party Monitoring

## 1. Quick concept

Continuous monitoring means reviewing risk when relevant signals or changes occur—not necessarily watching every supplier in real time. Monitoring depth should reflect criticality, access, data, and business exposure.

## 2. Useful triggers

- SOC 2 report period expires or a certification/report becomes due for review.
- Vendor has a security incident or material outage.
- Service introduces a new subprocessor, region, data type, integration, or privileged-access route.
- Contract renewal or ownership change occurs.
- Remediation deadline is missed.
- Regulatory/contractual obligations or service criticality change.
- External rating/threat intelligence produces a signal requiring validation.

External ratings are indicators, not proof. Validate material signals with the vendor and relevant technical owners before making consequential decisions.

## 3. Operating workflow

1. Assign vendor tier and business owner.
2. Define triggers, review cadence, and escalation thresholds.
3. Link supplier inventory with Procurement, Legal, Security, and service workflows.
4. Triage signals for relevance and false positives.
5. Request clarification and assess impact.
6. Record findings, decisions, conditions, and deadlines.
7. Reassess, restrict, or exit the service according to the organization's process.

## 4. Dashboard metrics

Critical vendors overdue for reassessment; high-risk findings past due; reports due to expire; unresolved incident follow-ups; suppliers missing required contract notification terms; exceptions nearing expiry; and vendors without accountable business owners.

## 5. Interview questions

**Q: Does continuous monitoring mean scanning vendors every day?** Not necessarily. It means monitoring is ongoing and proportionate, using relevant triggers and review cadences.

**Q: What do you do after a vendor rating drops?** Validate what changed, assess relevance to the specific service, request corroborating evidence, contact the vendor where appropriate, assess business impact, and route the decision to the proper risk owner. Avoid automatic conclusions from an opaque score.
